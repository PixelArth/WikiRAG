Earth is the third planet from the Sun and the only place in the Universe known to host life. It is the largest and densest of the four rocky planets. About 71% of its surface is covered by liquid water, which gives it the nickname "the Blue Planet". Earth formed about 4.54 billion years ago and has one natural satellite, the Moon.

## Quick facts

- Diameter: about 12,742 km on average (12,756 km at the equator)
- Average distance from the Sun: 149.6 million km (1 astronomical unit)
- Orbital period (year): 365.25 days
- Rotation period: 23 hours 56 minutes (a solar day lasts 24 hours)
- Axial tilt: 23.4 degrees
- Surface gravity: 9.8 m/s²
- Mass: about 5.97 × 10^24 kg
- Mean density: 5.51 g/cm³, the highest of any planet in the Solar System
- Mean surface temperature: about 15 °C
- Moons: 1

## Atmosphere

Earth's atmosphere is about 78% nitrogen, 21% oxygen and nearly 1% argon, with small amounts of carbon dioxide, water vapour and other gases. The oxygen was produced over billions of years by living organisms. The atmosphere shields the surface from most harmful ultraviolet light and from many meteoroids, it keeps the surface warm through a natural greenhouse effect, and it carries the water cycle and the weather. About 99% of the atmosphere lies within 32 km of the surface.

## Interior and magnetic field

Earth has four main layers:

- The crust is a thin rocky shell, about 5 to 10 km thick under the oceans and 30 to 70 km thick under the continents.
- The mantle is a thick layer of hot rock that flows very slowly over millions of years.
- The outer core is made of liquid iron and nickel.
- The inner core is a solid ball of iron and nickel, about 1,220 km in radius, kept solid by the pressure.

The movement of liquid iron in the outer core generates Earth's magnetic field. This field forms the magnetosphere, a region around the planet that deflects most of the charged particles of the solar wind and produces the auroras near the poles.

## Surface and plate tectonics

The outer shell of Earth is broken into large pieces called tectonic plates, which move a few centimetres per year. Their motion builds mountains, causes earthquakes, and drives volcanoes, mostly along the plate boundaries. Earth is the only planet in the Solar System known to have active plate tectonics. Oceans hold about 97% of Earth's water, and the rest is found in ice caps, glaciers, groundwater, lakes and the atmosphere. The highest point on land is Mount Everest, at about 8,849 m above sea level, and the deepest known point in the oceans is the Challenger Deep, at about 10,900 m below sea level.

## Seasons and orbit

Earth's axis is tilted by 23.4 degrees compared with its orbit. This tilt, and not the changing distance from the Sun, causes the seasons: each hemisphere receives more direct sunlight in summer, when it is tilted toward the Sun, and less in winter. Earth takes 365.25 days to orbit the Sun, which is why a leap day is added to the calendar roughly every four years. Its orbit is almost circular, and the distance from the Sun varies by only about 3% over the year.

## Earth as a world among others

- Earth is the only planet not named after a Greek or Roman god; its name comes from Old English and Germanic words for "ground".
- Life appeared on Earth at least 3.5 billion years ago, and it is the only place known to have life so far.
- The famous "Blue Marble" photograph of Earth was taken by the Apollo 17 crew in December 1972, on their way to the Moon.
- Satellites in orbit now observe Earth continuously, measuring weather, oceans, ice and vegetation.
