Uranus is the seventh planet from the Sun and the third largest in diameter. It is an ice giant with a pale blue-green colour, and it is the planet with the strangest tilt: it rotates on its side, with an axis tilted by almost 98 degrees. Uranus was the first planet discovered with a telescope.

## Quick facts

- Diameter: 51,118 km, about 4 times the diameter of Earth
- Mass: about 14.5 times the mass of Earth
- Average distance from the Sun: 2.87 billion km (19.2 astronomical units)
- Orbital period (year): 84 Earth years
- Rotation period: 17 hours 14 minutes, in the retrograde direction
- Axial tilt: 97.8 degrees
- Gravity at the 1-bar level: 8.7 m/s²
- Mean temperature at the 1-bar level: about −195 °C
- Moons: 29 known (as of 2025)
- Rings: 13 known rings

## Atmosphere and composition

The atmosphere of Uranus is made of about 83% hydrogen, 15% helium and 2% methane. The methane absorbs red light and gives the planet its blue-green colour. Underneath the atmosphere lies a thick, hot mantle of water, ammonia and methane, usually called ices, which is why Uranus and Neptune are called ice giants and not gas giants. At the centre there is a small rocky core. Uranus has the coldest atmosphere of any planet, with a minimum temperature of about 49 K (−224 °C), even though Neptune is much farther from the Sun. Unlike the other giant planets, Uranus gives off almost no internal heat.

## Tilt and seasons

Uranus is tilted by 97.8 degrees, so it orbits the Sun lying almost on its side. One cause may be a giant collision long ago. As a result each pole spends about 42 years in continuous sunlight, followed by 42 years of darkness, and each season lasts about 21 years. Its magnetic field is also unusual: it is tilted by about 59 degrees from the rotation axis and does not pass through the planet's centre.

## Rings and moons

Uranus has 13 known rings, narrow and dark, first detected in 1977 when they passed in front of a star. Uranus has 29 known moons, which are named after characters from the works of William Shakespeare and Alexander Pope, and not after figures from classical mythology. The five largest are Miranda, Ariel, Umbriel, Titania and Oberon. Titania is the largest, with a diameter of about 1,578 km. Miranda has a strange, patchwork surface with cliffs about 20 km high, one of the highest in the Solar System.

## Discovery

William Herschel discovered Uranus on 13 March 1781 using a telescope in Bath, England. He first thought it was a comet. After other astronomers calculated its orbit, it was recognised as a new planet, the first planet found since antiquity. Herschel wanted to call it Georgium Sidus, after King George III, but the name Uranus, after the Greek god of the sky, became standard.

## Exploration

Voyager 2 is the only spacecraft that has visited Uranus. It flew past on 24 January 1986, at a distance of about 81,500 km from the cloud tops. It discovered ten new moons and two new rings, and measured the magnetic field. A Uranus orbiter and atmospheric probe was recommended as the highest priority new mission for NASA in the US planetary science decadal survey published in 2022.
