Titan is the largest moon of Saturn and the second largest moon in the Solar System, after Jupiter's Ganymede. It is the only moon with a dense atmosphere, and the only world other than Earth known to have stable liquid on its surface, in the form of lakes and seas of liquid methane and ethane.

## Quick facts

- Diameter: 5,150 km, larger than the planet Mercury (4,879 km)
- Average distance from Saturn: about 1.22 million km
- Orbital period: 15.95 Earth days
- Rotation: locked to its orbit, so the same face always points to Saturn
- Surface temperature: about −179 °C
- Atmosphere: about 95% nitrogen and 5% methane
- Surface pressure: about 1.5 times the pressure on Earth
- Discovered: 1655, by Christiaan Huygens

## Atmosphere

Titan's atmosphere is made mostly of nitrogen, like Earth's, with a few percent of methane and traces of hydrogen and complex organic compounds. It is thicker than Earth's: the pressure at the surface is about 1.5 times higher, and the atmosphere extends much higher into space. An orange haze of organic particles, produced when sunlight breaks up methane, hides the surface from view at visible wavelengths. The thick haze acts as a kind of umbrella that blocks some sunlight, and it also traps some heat, giving the surface a temperature of about −179 °C.

## Surface and methane cycle

On Earth, water evaporates, forms clouds and falls as rain. On Titan, methane plays the same role. Methane clouds form in the atmosphere, rain falls, rivers carve channels and liquid collects in lakes and seas.

- The seas and lakes are located mostly near the poles. The largest, Kraken Mare, is comparable in area to the Caspian Sea on Earth.
- Near the equator there are vast fields of dunes, made of dark grains of organic material.
- The surface is hard, and made of water ice that is as strong as rock at such low temperatures.

## Interior

Evidence from Cassini's measurements of gravity and tides suggests that Titan has a global ocean of liquid water, probably salty, hidden under a crust of ice some tens of kilometres below the surface. Deeper still, the interior is made of high-pressure ice and a rocky core.

## Exploration

- Pioneer 11 and the Voyager probes flew by in 1979, 1980 and 1981. Voyager 1 passed close to Titan in November 1980, but the haze hid the surface.
- The Cassini orbiter flew by Titan more than a hundred times between 2004 and 2017, using radar and infrared instruments to see through the haze.
- The Huygens probe, carried by Cassini, parachuted through the atmosphere and landed on Titan on 14 January 2005. It was the first landing on a world in the outer Solar System, and it kept transmitting images and data from the surface for about 72 minutes.
- Dragonfly is a NASA mission with a car-sized, nuclear-powered rotorcraft that will fly from place to place on Titan. It is planned to launch in the late 2020s and arrive in the mid-2030s.

## Notable facts

- Titan is the only moon in the Solar System known to have clouds and weather similar to Earth's, although the liquid is methane and not water.
- With the gravity of only about 14% of Earth's, a person could in principle fly in Titan's thick atmosphere by flapping wings attached to the arms.
