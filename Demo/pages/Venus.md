Venus is the second planet from the Sun and the hottest planet in the Solar System. It is almost the same size as Earth, which is why it is sometimes called Earth's sister planet, but its thick carbon dioxide atmosphere has created a runaway greenhouse effect and a surface hot enough to melt lead. After the Sun and the Moon, Venus is the brightest natural object in the sky.

## Quick facts

- Diameter: 12,104 km, about 95% of Earth's diameter
- Average distance from the Sun: 108.2 million km (0.72 astronomical units)
- Orbital period (year): 224.7 Earth days
- Rotation period: 243 Earth days, in the retrograde (backward) direction
- Length of a solar day: about 117 Earth days
- Surface gravity: 8.9 m/s²
- Surface temperature: about 465 °C
- Surface pressure: about 92 times the pressure at Earth's surface
- Moons: none
- Rings: none

## Atmosphere

The atmosphere of Venus is about 96.5% carbon dioxide and 3.5% nitrogen, with traces of sulphur dioxide and other gases. The pressure at the surface is about 92 times that of Earth, similar to the pressure found about 900 metres under Earth's oceans. High in the atmosphere, between roughly 45 and 70 km, are thick clouds of sulphuric acid droplets that reflect most of the sunlight, which makes Venus bright and hides its surface from ordinary telescopes.

The carbon dioxide traps heat in a runaway greenhouse effect. As a result, the surface of Venus is about 465 °C everywhere, day and night, hotter than the surface of Mercury even though Venus is almost twice as far from the Sun.

## Surface and interior

Radar mapping shows that most of Venus is covered by volcanic plains. The planet has thousands of volcanoes and large lava flows, and some scientists think volcanic activity may continue today. There are two main highland regions, Ishtar Terra in the north and Aphrodite Terra near the equator. The highest mountains are Maxwell Montes, in Ishtar Terra, rising about 11 km above the average surface level. Venus has few impact craters, which suggests that its surface is geologically young, probably a few hundred million years old. Venus has no global magnetic field. Its interior is thought to resemble Earth's, with an iron core, a mantle and a crust.

## Rotation and orbit

Venus rotates very slowly and in the opposite direction to most planets, so the Sun rises in the west and sets in the east. One rotation takes 243 Earth days, longer than the 224.7 days the planet needs to orbit the Sun, so a day on Venus is longer than its year. Its orbit is the closest to a perfect circle of any planet. Its axis is tilted by only about 3 degrees, so Venus has no real seasons.

## Exploration

- Mariner 2, launched by NASA, made the first successful flyby of another planet when it passed Venus in December 1962.
- Venera 7, launched by the Soviet Union, made the first successful landing on another planet in December 1970. It transmitted for about 23 minutes.
- Venera 13 landed in 1982 and sent the first colour images of the surface of Venus. It survived about two hours in the heat.
- Magellan, launched by NASA, orbited Venus from 1990 to 1994 and used radar to map about 98% of the surface.
- Several other orbiters and flybys, including ESA's Venus Express and JAXA's Akatsuki, studied the atmosphere. New missions, such as NASA's DAVINCI and VERITAS and ESA's EnVision, have been selected to return to Venus.

## Notable facts

- Venus is called the "morning star" or the "evening star" because it shines brightly near the horizon just before sunrise or after sunset. It is a planet, not a star.
- Venus shows phases like the Moon when seen through a telescope. Galileo's observation of the phases in 1610 supported the idea that planets orbit the Sun.
- Venus spins so slowly that its equator turns at only about 6.5 km/h, compared with about 1,670 km/h for Earth's equator.
