Ganymede is the largest moon in the Solar System and the third of Jupiter's four Galilean moons. It is bigger than the planet Mercury, although it has only about half of Mercury's mass because it is made of rock and ice. Ganymede is the only moon known to generate its own magnetic field.

## Quick facts

- Diameter: 5,268 km, larger than Mercury (4,879 km)
- Mass: roughly half of Mercury's mass
- Average distance from Jupiter: about 1,070,400 km
- Orbital period: 7.15 Earth days
- Rotation: locked to its orbit, so the same face always points to Jupiter
- Composition: about equal parts rock and water ice
- Atmosphere: a very thin oxygen atmosphere
- Discovered: January 1610, by Galileo Galilei

## Composition and interior

Ganymede is made of roughly equal parts of rock and water ice. It has a differentiated interior with a metallic iron core, a rocky mantle and a thick outer layer of ice. Scientists believe there is a large ocean of salty water about 150 km or more below the surface, sandwiched between layers of ice. This ocean may hold more water than all of Earth's oceans, and the layers of ice above and below it may keep it separated from the rocky seafloor.

## Surface

The surface of Ganymede has two main kinds of terrain. About a third is dark, very old, heavily cratered terrain. The rest is lighter, younger terrain, crossed by networks of grooves and ridges that probably formed when the crust was stretched and cracked. Bright rays surround fresh craters, as water ice thrown out of the ground reflects more light than the dark surface.

## Magnetic field

Ganymede is the only moon in the Solar System known to have its own magnetic field, probably produced by movement of molten iron in its core. The field creates a small magnetosphere inside the much larger magnetosphere of Jupiter. This produces auroras around its poles, and the way those auroras move offered additional evidence of the subsurface ocean.

## Orbital resonance

Ganymede, Europa and Io are linked by a resonance. For every orbit of Ganymede, Europa completes two and Io completes four. This keeps their orbits slightly eccentric and causes tidal flexing, which heats Io and Europa most strongly. Ganymede feels a weaker effect, since it is farther from Jupiter.

## Exploration

- Pioneer 10 and 11, Voyager 1 and 2 and Galileo observed Ganymede in the 1970s, 1980s and 1990s. Galileo discovered its magnetic field in 1996.
- Juno flew within about 1,038 km of Ganymede on 7 June 2021, the closest approach by a spacecraft in more than twenty years, and returned detailed images.
- ESA's JUICE mission, launched in April 2023, is due to reach the Jupiter system in 2031. It is planned to enter orbit around Ganymede in December 2034, which would make it the first spacecraft to orbit a moon other than Earth's.
