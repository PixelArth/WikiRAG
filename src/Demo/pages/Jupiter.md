Jupiter is the fifth planet from the Sun and by far the largest planet in the Solar System. It is a gas giant, mostly hydrogen and helium, with a mass more than two and a half times that of all the other planets combined. Jupiter is known for its colourful cloud bands, the Great Red Spot, a powerful magnetic field and a large family of moons, including the four large Galilean moons.

## Quick facts

- Equatorial diameter: 142,984 km, about 11 times the diameter of Earth
- Mass: about 318 times the mass of Earth
- Average distance from the Sun: 778.5 million km (5.2 astronomical units)
- Orbital period (year): 11.86 Earth years
- Rotation period: 9 hours 56 minutes, the shortest day of any planet
- Gravity at the 1-bar level: 23.1 m/s²
- Mean temperature at the 1-bar level: about −110 °C
- Moons: 95 confirmed (2023 count)
- Rings: a faint ring system

## Composition and structure

Jupiter is made mostly of hydrogen (about 90% of its atoms) and helium (about 10%), the same two elements that make up the Sun, with small amounts of methane, ammonia and water. It has no solid surface. Deeper in the planet the pressure becomes so high that hydrogen behaves like a liquid metal. Jupiter may have a dense core, possibly diluted and mixed with the surrounding layers. Its fast rotation flattens the planet slightly at the poles. Jupiter gives off more heat than it receives from the Sun, a leftover of its formation and slow contraction.

## Atmosphere and the Great Red Spot

Jupiter's visible atmosphere is arranged in light zones and dark belts, stretched along lines of latitude by fast winds. Storms and clouds of ammonia ice and other compounds give it its colours.

The Great Red Spot is a giant storm, a high-pressure system that has been observed for well over a century. In 2017 it measured about 16,350 km across, about 1.3 times the diameter of Earth, but it has been shrinking for decades. In the late 19th century it was more than twice as wide. Winds at its edge blow at several hundred kilometres per hour.

## Magnetic field and rings

Jupiter has the strongest magnetic field of any planet, which traps charged particles in intense radiation belts. The field creates the brightest auroras in the Solar System, near the poles. The planet has a faint system of thin dusty rings, discovered by the Voyager 1 spacecraft in 1979.

## Moons

Jupiter has 95 confirmed moons according to the 2023 count, and most of them are small and distant. The four largest, the Galilean moons, were discovered by Galileo Galilei in January 1610 and are among the most interesting worlds of the Solar System:

- Io is the most volcanically active body in the Solar System.
- Europa has a salty ocean beneath an icy crust.
- Ganymede is the largest moon in the Solar System.
- Callisto has an ancient, heavily cratered surface.

## Exploration

- Pioneer 10 made the first flyby of Jupiter in December 1973, followed by Pioneer 11.
- Voyager 1 and Voyager 2 flew past in 1979 and discovered the rings and the volcanoes of Io.
- Galileo, launched by NASA, orbited Jupiter from 1995 to 2003 and released the first probe to enter the atmosphere of an outer planet, in December 1995.
- Juno, launched by NASA, has orbited Jupiter since July 2016, on a path that passes over the poles. It maps the gravity and the magnetic field, and studies the deep atmosphere.
- Europa Clipper, launched by NASA on 14 October 2024, is due to reach Jupiter in April 2030 to study Europa.
- JUICE, an ESA mission launched in April 2023, is due to arrive in 2031 to study Ganymede, Europa and Callisto.
