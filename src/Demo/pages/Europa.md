Europa is the smallest of the four Galilean moons of Jupiter and one of the most promising places in the Solar System to search for life beyond Earth. Its smooth, icy surface hides a deep global ocean of salty liquid water, kept warm by the tides raised by Jupiter.

## Quick facts

- Diameter: 3,122 km, slightly smaller than Earth's Moon
- Average distance from Jupiter: about 671,000 km
- Orbital period: 3.55 Earth days
- Rotation: locked to its orbit, so the same face always points to Jupiter
- Surface: water ice, very smooth and young
- Ice shell thickness: estimated at roughly 15 to 25 km
- Ocean: estimated at 60 to 150 km deep, with about twice the water of all of Earth's oceans
- Atmosphere: extremely thin, made mostly of oxygen
- Discovered: January 1610, by Galileo Galilei

## Ice shell and surface

Europa's surface is one of the smoothest in the Solar System, with very few craters. This means that it is young, probably between 40 and 90 million years old on average, and that the ice shell is constantly renewed by the movement of the ice. The surface is crossed by long cracks and ridges called lineae, which look brownish because of salts and other material mixed into the ice, and by jumbled regions called chaos terrain, where the ice seems to have broken up and refrozen. Scientists think that some of these features formed where water from below rose through the shell.

## Subsurface ocean

Beneath the ice shell lies an ocean of liquid water. Evidence for it came from the magnetometer of the Galileo spacecraft, which detected a magnetic signal that fits a layer of salty water below the surface. The ocean is estimated to be 60 to 150 km deep and contains roughly twice as much water as all of Earth's oceans put together, even though Europa is smaller than the Moon. The heat that keeps the water liquid comes from tidal flexing, and probably also from the rocky seafloor.

## Plumes and habitability

Observations by the Hubble Space Telescope in 2012 and later years suggest that plumes of water vapour may sometimes erupt through the ice from the south polar region. If they exist, a spacecraft could sample the ocean's water without landing. For life as we know it, the necessary ingredients are liquid water, energy and chemical elements such as carbon, nitrogen, phosphorus and sulphur. Europa seems to have water and an energy source, and the other ingredients may be present as well. No evidence of life has been found.

## Exploration

- Pioneer 10 and 11 and the Voyager probes took the first images in the 1970s.
- The Galileo orbiter flew by Europa several times between 1995 and 2003, providing images and the magnetic data.
- Europa Clipper, a NASA mission launched on 14 October 2024 by a Falcon Heavy rocket, is due to reach the Jupiter system in April 2030. It will make dozens of close flybys of Europa, and will map the ice shell and ocean. It will not land and it is not designed to look for life directly, only to find out whether Europa is habitable.
- ESA's JUICE mission, launched in 2023, will also fly by Europa.
