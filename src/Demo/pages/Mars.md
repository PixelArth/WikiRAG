Mars is the fourth planet from the Sun and the second smallest planet in the Solar System. It is often called the Red Planet because of the iron oxide, or rust, in its dust. Mars is a cold, dry desert world with a thin atmosphere, but it has volcanoes, canyons and polar ice caps, and there is strong evidence that liquid water once flowed on its surface. It has been visited by more spacecraft than any other planet besides Earth.

## Quick facts

- Diameter: 6,779 km, a little more than half of Earth's diameter
- Average distance from the Sun: 227.9 million km (1.52 astronomical units)
- Orbital period (year): 687 Earth days
- Rotation period: 24 hours 37 minutes (a Martian solar day, called a sol, lasts 24 hours 40 minutes)
- Axial tilt: 25.2 degrees
- Surface gravity: 3.7 m/s², about 38% of Earth's
- Mean temperature: about −65 °C, ranging from about −140 °C to 20 °C
- Atmosphere: about 95% carbon dioxide, with a pressure under 1% of Earth's
- Moons: 2 (Phobos and Deimos)

## Atmosphere and climate

The Martian atmosphere is thin and made of about 95% carbon dioxide, 2.8% nitrogen and 2% argon. The pressure at the surface is only about 0.6% of Earth's, which is too low for liquid water to remain stable at the surface for long. The temperature drops very low at night and near the poles, and even in summer at the equator it rarely rises above 20 °C. Fine dust is lifted by the wind, and sometimes giant dust storms cover the whole planet for weeks. Mars has seasons similar to Earth's because of its axial tilt, but each season lasts about twice as long, since the Martian year is 687 days.

## Surface features

- Olympus Mons is a shield volcano about 21.9 km high and about 600 km wide, roughly two and a half times the height of Mount Everest above sea level. It is the tallest known volcano in the Solar System.
- Valles Marineris is a system of canyons about 4,000 km long and up to 7 km deep, a distance comparable to the width of the United States.
- The Tharsis region is a huge volcanic plateau that carries Olympus Mons and several other giant volcanoes.
- Hellas Planitia is a giant impact basin, about 2,300 km across, in the southern hemisphere.
- Both poles are covered by ice caps made of water ice and frozen carbon dioxide.

## Water and the search for life

Orbiters and rovers have found river valleys, ancient lake beds and minerals that only form in the presence of water. Billions of years ago Mars was warmer and wetter, with a thicker atmosphere. Today most of the water is frozen in the polar caps and in the ground. Scientists are searching for signs of past microbial life, and no evidence of life has been found yet.

## Moons

Mars has two small, irregularly shaped moons, probably captured asteroids or debris from a past impact.

- Phobos is about 22 km across and orbits only about 6,000 km above the surface, once every 7 hours 39 minutes. It is slowly spiralling toward Mars and will eventually break apart or crash in tens of millions of years.
- Deimos is about 12 km across and orbits farther away, once every 30.3 hours.

Both moons were discovered in 1877 by the American astronomer Asaph Hall.

## Exploration

- Mariner 4, launched by NASA, made the first successful flyby of Mars in 1965.
- The two Viking landers, launched by NASA, reached the surface in 1976 and operated for years.
- Sojourner, the first rover on Mars, landed in 1997 with the Pathfinder mission.
- The rovers Spirit and Opportunity landed in 2004. Opportunity worked for almost 15 years.
- Curiosity landed in Gale Crater in August 2012 and is still exploring.
- Perseverance landed in Jezero Crater on 18 February 2021. It carried the small helicopter Ingenuity, which made the first powered, controlled flight on another planet on 19 April 2021 and flew 72 times before the end of its mission in January 2024.
- China's Zhurong rover landed in 2021, as part of the Tianwen-1 mission.

Several orbiters around Mars, including NASA's MAVEN and Mars Reconnaissance Orbiter and ESA's Mars Express, study the atmosphere and the surface from space.
