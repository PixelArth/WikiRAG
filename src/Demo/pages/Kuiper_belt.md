The Kuiper belt is a vast, disk-shaped region of icy bodies that orbit the Sun beyond Neptune. It is similar to the asteroid belt, but it is much larger, about 20 times wider and many times more massive, and its objects are made mostly of frozen volatiles such as water, methane and ammonia. The belt is home to several dwarf planets, including Pluto.

## Quick facts

- Location: beyond the orbit of Neptune
- Distance from the Sun: from about 30 to about 50 astronomical units (roughly 4.5 to 7.5 billion km)
- Composition: ices of water, methane and ammonia mixed with rock
- Dwarf planets: Pluto, Eris, Haumea and Makemake
- Discovered: the first object after Pluto was found in 1992
- Source of: short-period comets
- Visited by: New Horizons, in 2015 (Pluto) and 2019 (Arrokoth)

## Discovery

In the 1940s and 1950s, the astronomers Kenneth Edgeworth and Gerard Kuiper suggested that a belt of small icy bodies might exist beyond Neptune. For decades nothing was found except Pluto and its moon Charon. In August 1992, David Jewitt and Jane Luu discovered the first object, 1992 QB1, using a telescope on Mauna Kea in Hawaii. Since then, thousands of Kuiper belt objects have been catalogued. The belt is sometimes called the Edgeworth–Kuiper belt.

## Structure

Most Kuiper belt objects belong to one of three groups:

- The classical belt contains objects on nearly circular orbits, between about 42 and 48 astronomical units from the Sun.
- Resonant objects are locked in an orbital resonance with Neptune. The plutinos, which include Pluto, orbit twice for every three orbits of Neptune.
- The scattered disc contains objects pushed onto very elongated and tilted orbits by Neptune's gravity.

Beyond about 48 astronomical units the number of objects falls sharply, a feature known as the Kuiper cliff. The belt should not be confused with the Oort cloud, a hypothetical spherical shell of comets that lies far beyond it, thousands of astronomical units from the Sun.

## Dwarf planets

Pluto and Eris are the largest known objects in the belt. Eris, discovered in 2005, has a diameter of about 2,326 km, slightly less than Pluto's 2,377 km, but it is about 27% more massive. Its discovery was one of the reasons for the reclassification of Pluto in 2006. Haumea and Makemake are two other dwarf planets in the Kuiper belt.

## Comets and Neptune's moon

The Kuiper belt is the source of the short-period comets, which take less than 200 years to orbit the Sun, such as Halley's Comet. The gravity of Neptune sometimes sends a Kuiper belt object toward the inner Solar System. Neptune's large moon Triton is probably a former Kuiper belt object that was captured.

## Exploration

New Horizons crossed the belt after its flyby of Pluto. On 1 January 2019 it passed the object Arrokoth, at a distance of about 3,500 km. Arrokoth is a contact binary about 35 km long, made of two lobes that gently merged early in the history of the Solar System. It is the most distant object explored by a spacecraft up to now, and it is thought to be well preserved, because it has stayed in the cold for billions of years.
