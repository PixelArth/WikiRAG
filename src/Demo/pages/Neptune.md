Neptune is the eighth and farthest known planet from the Sun. It is an ice giant, slightly smaller than Uranus but more massive. Neptune is dark, cold and windy, and has the fastest winds measured on any planet in the Solar System. It is the only planet that was found by mathematical prediction before being seen.

## Quick facts

- Diameter: 49,244 km, almost 4 times the diameter of Earth
- Mass: about 17 times the mass of Earth
- Average distance from the Sun: 4.5 billion km (30.1 astronomical units)
- Orbital period (year): 164.8 Earth years
- Rotation period: 16 hours 6 minutes
- Gravity at the 1-bar level: 11.0 m/s²
- Mean temperature at the 1-bar level: about −200 °C
- Moons: 16 known (as of 2024)
- Rings: 5 main rings

## Atmosphere and weather

Neptune's atmosphere is made mainly of hydrogen and helium, with a small amount of methane. The methane absorbs red light and contributes to the planet's vivid blue colour, though other, still unidentified, compounds probably add to the intense shade. The weather is extreme. Winds in the upper atmosphere reach about 2,100 km/h, the fastest in the Solar System, and they blow in the direction opposite to the planet's rotation near the equator. Neptune receives only about one-thousandth of the sunlight that Earth gets and nonetheless radiates more heat than it absorbs, so it has an internal source of energy that helps to power the winds.

Voyager 2 saw a huge dark storm, the Great Dark Spot, in 1989. When the Hubble Space Telescope looked for it in 1994, it had disappeared, and other dark spots have appeared and faded since then.

## Discovery

By the 1840s, astronomers noticed that Uranus did not follow exactly its predicted orbit, and suspected the gravity of another planet. Urbain Le Verrier in France and John Couch Adams in England both calculated where this planet should be. Johann Galle of the Berlin Observatory looked at the position sent by Le Verrier and found Neptune on 23 September 1846, within about one degree of the prediction. Because of its slow orbit, Neptune completed its first full orbit since its discovery in 2011.

## Moons and rings

The largest moon is Triton, with a diameter of about 2,707 km. Triton orbits Neptune in the retrograde direction, opposite to the planet's rotation, which suggests that it is a Kuiper belt object captured by Neptune's gravity long ago. It is very cold, with a surface temperature of about −235 °C, and Voyager 2 saw dark plumes of nitrogen gas rising from its surface. Neptune has 16 known moons in total, according to the count of 2024. Neptune also has a system of faint, dark rings, which are made of dust and which have clumps called arcs.

## Exploration

Voyager 2 is the only spacecraft that has visited Neptune. It made its closest approach on 25 August 1989, flying about 4,800 km above the north pole, and then left the planets behind. It discovered six new moons, the rings and the Great Dark Spot, and revealed the geysers on Triton. The Hubble Space Telescope and the James Webb Space Telescope now monitor the planet from space, and no new mission to Neptune has been approved so far.
