A comet is a small body of ice, dust and rock that orbits the Sun. When a comet gets close to the Sun, the ice heats up and turns into gas, and the comet develops a glowing cloud around its nucleus and one or more long tails. Comets are leftovers from the formation of the Solar System about 4.6 billion years ago, and they have been well preserved in the cold outer regions.

## Quick facts

- Nucleus: a solid body of ice and dust, usually from under 1 km to a few tens of kilometres across
- Parts: nucleus, coma and tails
- Short-period comets: orbital period under 200 years, coming from the Kuiper belt
- Long-period comets: orbital period over 200 years, coming from the Oort cloud
- Best-known comet: Halley's Comet, with a period of about 76 years

## Structure

- The nucleus is a solid body, sometimes called a dirty snowball, made of water ice, frozen gases such as carbon dioxide, and dust. Its surface is very dark.
- The coma is a huge cloud of gas and dust that forms around the nucleus when it is warmed by the Sun. It can be larger than a planet.
- The ion tail is a thin, straight tail of electrically charged gas, which glows blue and is pushed away by the solar wind.
- The dust tail is a broad, curved and yellowish tail, made of dust that is pushed by sunlight.

The tails always point away from the Sun, whichever direction the comet is moving. When a comet moves away from the Sun, its tail therefore goes in front of it.

## Origin and orbits

Short-period comets, whose orbits take less than 200 years, come from the Kuiper belt beyond Neptune. Long-period comets come from the Oort cloud, a hypothetical, distant shell of icy bodies that may surround the Solar System at thousands of astronomical units from the Sun. A comet's orbit is generally very elongated, bringing it close to the Sun and then far into the cold again. Each time a comet passes the Sun it loses material, so that comets slowly wear out, and some break up or disappear completely.

## Halley's Comet

Halley's Comet is the best-known comet. Its orbit takes about 76 years. Edmond Halley, in 1705, recognised that comets seen in 1531, 1607 and 1682 were the same object, and he predicted that it would return in 1758. It did, after his death. The comet was last at its closest point to the Sun on 9 February 1986, and the next time will be on 28 July 2061. Its nucleus is a potato-shaped body about 15 km long. Debris left along its orbit causes two meteor showers each year, the Eta Aquariids in May and the Orionids in October.

## Exploration

- In March 1986 ESA's Giotto spacecraft flew within about 600 km of the nucleus of Halley's Comet and took the first close pictures of a comet's nucleus.
- NASA's Stardust mission flew through the coma of comet Wild 2 and returned dust samples to Earth in January 2006.
- ESA's Rosetta mission reached comet 67P/Churyumov–Gerasimenko in August 2014 and orbited it for two years. In November 2014 its lander Philae made the first soft landing on the nucleus of a comet. The mission ended on 30 September 2016, when Rosetta was gently crashed onto the comet. The nucleus of 67P is about 4 km across and the comet orbits the Sun every 6.4 years.

## Notable comets

Comet Hale–Bopp, discovered in 1995, was visible to the naked eye for about 18 months in 1996 and 1997, a record. Comets are seen at irregular times, and a very bright comet can appear with little warning.
