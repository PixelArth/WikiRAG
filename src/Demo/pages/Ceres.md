Ceres is the largest object in the asteroid belt between Mars and Jupiter, and the only dwarf planet in the inner Solar System. It was the first asteroid ever discovered, in 1801. Ceres is a small, round, icy world with a surface of dark rock, clays and salts, and it holds a large part of the mass of the whole belt.

## Quick facts

- Diameter: about 940 km, about a quarter of the Moon's diameter
- Average distance from the Sun: about 414 million km (2.77 astronomical units)
- Orbital period (year): 4.6 Earth years
- Rotation period: about 9 hours
- Surface gravity: 0.28 m/s², about 3% of Earth's
- Share of the asteroid belt's mass: roughly one third (estimates range from about a quarter to about 40%)
- Discovered: 1 January 1801, by Giuseppe Piazzi
- Visited by: the Dawn spacecraft (2015 to 2018)

## Discovery and classification

Giuseppe Piazzi, an Italian astronomer, discovered Ceres from Palermo in Sicily on 1 January 1801, in the gap between Mars and Jupiter where astronomers expected to find a missing planet. He named it after Ceres, the Roman goddess of agriculture. Ceres was considered a planet for about fifty years. After many more objects were found in the same region in the 1840s and 1850s, it was reclassified as an asteroid. In 2006, when the International Astronomical Union created the category of dwarf planets, Ceres was included because it is massive enough to be round.

## Composition and interior

Ceres is about 940 km across, so it is much smaller than Pluto, but it is round. Measurements by Dawn suggest that Ceres has a rocky core and a mantle rich in water ice, covered by a dusty crust a few tens of kilometres thick. The surface contains clay minerals and ammonia compounds, and there is evidence of a layer of salty brine below the surface in some places. Ceres has water ice in permanently shadowed craters near its poles, and traces of water vapour have been detected around it.

## Bright spots and features

When Dawn approached Ceres in 2015, it revealed mysterious bright spots on the floor of Occator Crater, which is about 92 km wide. The spots are deposits of sodium carbonate, a salt, left behind when salty water from below rose to the surface and evaporated. Ahuna Mons, about 4 km high, is a mountain that appears to be a cryovolcano, a volcano that erupts a mixture of water and salts instead of molten rock.

## Exploration

Dawn, a NASA mission launched on 27 September 2007, was the first spacecraft to orbit two different bodies beyond the Earth–Moon system. It orbited the asteroid Vesta from 2011 to 2012, then travelled to Ceres and entered orbit in March 2015, becoming the first spacecraft to visit a dwarf planet. The mission ended in 2018 when Dawn ran out of the fuel needed to control its orientation. It remains in orbit around Ceres and will continue to circle the dwarf planet for decades.
