Pluto is a dwarf planet in the Kuiper belt, a region of icy bodies beyond the orbit of Neptune. It was the ninth planet from 1930 until 2006, when the International Astronomical Union reclassified it. Pluto is smaller than Earth's Moon and has five known moons, the largest of which, Charon, is so big that the two bodies are sometimes described as a double system.

## Quick facts

- Diameter: 2,377 km, about two-thirds of the Moon's diameter
- Average distance from the Sun: 5.9 billion km (39.5 astronomical units)
- Orbital period (year): 248 Earth years
- Rotation period: 6.4 Earth days, in the retrograde direction
- Axial tilt: about 120 degrees
- Surface gravity: 0.6 m/s², about 6% of Earth's
- Mean temperature: about −225 °C
- Moons: 5 (Charon, Styx, Nix, Kerberos and Hydra)
- Discovered: 1930, by Clyde Tombaugh

## Discovery and reclassification

Clyde Tombaugh found Pluto in February 1930 at the Lowell Observatory in Flagstaff, Arizona, by comparing photographs of the same part of the sky taken some days apart. It was the first object found beyond Neptune. Over the following decades, Pluto turned out to be much smaller than first thought, and from 1992 astronomers began to discover many other icy bodies in the same region, including Eris, which is almost the same size as Pluto.

On 24 August 2006, the International Astronomical Union defined a planet as a body that orbits the Sun, is massive enough to be nearly round, and has cleared the neighbourhood around its orbit of other objects. Pluto meets the first two conditions but not the third, because it shares its region with many other objects. Pluto was therefore reclassified as a dwarf planet.

## Orbit

Pluto's orbit is elongated and tilted by about 17 degrees from the plane of the planets. Its distance from the Sun varies from about 29.7 astronomical units to about 49.3 astronomical units, so that for about 20 years in each orbit it is closer to the Sun than Neptune. Pluto and Neptune never collide, because they are locked in a 3:2 orbital resonance: Pluto orbits twice for every three orbits of Neptune, and the two are never close together.

## Surface and atmosphere

New Horizons showed a surprisingly varied world.

- Tombaugh Regio is a large bright region shaped like a heart. Its left half is Sputnik Planitia, a plain of nitrogen ice about 1,000 km wide, with no craters, which means that it is very young and is slowly renewed by convection in the ice.
- Mountains made of water ice, which behaves like rock at such cold temperatures, rise up to about 3,400 metres.
- Dark reddish regions are coloured by tholins, organic compounds formed when sunlight and cosmic rays change methane.

Pluto has a thin atmosphere, mostly nitrogen with some methane and carbon monoxide. It is thickest when Pluto is nearest to the Sun, and it freezes and falls to the surface as frost when Pluto moves away.

## Charon and the other moons

Charon, discovered in 1978, has a diameter of about 1,212 km, roughly half that of Pluto. It is so massive compared with Pluto that the centre of mass of the two bodies lies outside Pluto, in the space between them, so both orbit a point in space. Each always shows the same face to the other. Pluto's four smaller moons, Styx, Nix, Kerberos and Hydra, were found between 2005 and 2012 and are small and irregular.

## Exploration

NASA's New Horizons spacecraft was launched on 19 January 2006 and flew past Pluto on 14 July 2015, at a distance of about 12,500 km. It is the only spacecraft to have visited the system. Afterwards, it continued into the Kuiper belt and flew by the object Arrokoth on 1 January 2019.
