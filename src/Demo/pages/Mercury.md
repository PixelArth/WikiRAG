Mercury is the smallest planet in the Solar System and the closest to the Sun. It is only slightly larger than Earth's Moon. It has a very short year, extreme temperature swings and a heavily cratered surface that looks much like the Moon's. Mercury has no moons and no rings.

## Quick facts

- Diameter: 4,879 km, about 38% of Earth's diameter
- Average distance from the Sun: 57.9 million km (0.39 astronomical units)
- Orbital period (year): 88 Earth days
- Rotation period: 58.6 Earth days
- Length of a solar day (sunrise to sunrise): 176 Earth days
- Surface gravity: 3.7 m/s²
- Mean temperature: about 167 °C
- Surface temperature range: about −180 °C to 430 °C
- Moons: none
- Atmosphere: a very thin exosphere

## Surface and interior

Mercury's surface is covered with impact craters, plains of old lava and long cliffs called scarps. The largest feature is the Caloris Basin, an impact crater about 1,550 km across. Mercury is one of the densest planets, about 5.4 g/cm³. Its large metallic core takes up about 85% of the planet's radius, a larger share than in any other planet. Over billions of years the core has cooled and the planet has shrunk, wrinkling the crust into scarps that can be hundreds of kilometres long.

## Orbit and rotation

Mercury has the most elliptical orbit of the eight planets. Its distance from the Sun varies from about 46 million km at its closest to about 70 million km at its farthest. It travels at an average speed of about 47 km/s, faster than any other planet, which is why it was named after the swift messenger of the Roman gods.

Mercury is locked in a 3:2 spin–orbit resonance: it rotates exactly three times on its axis for every two orbits around the Sun. The result is that a day on Mercury, from one sunrise to the next, lasts 176 Earth days, twice as long as its 88-day year. Its axis is almost upright, with a tilt of less than 1 degree, so it has no seasons.

## Temperature and exosphere

Mercury has almost no atmosphere to hold heat. Only a thin exosphere exists, made of atoms blasted off the surface by sunlight and the solar wind. Daytime temperatures reach about 430 °C and fall to about −180 °C at night, one of the largest swings of any planet. Mercury is not the hottest planet, though: Venus is hotter because its thick atmosphere traps heat.

Radar and spacecraft observations show that water ice is present in permanently shadowed craters near Mercury's poles, where sunlight never reaches the crater floors and the temperature stays far below freezing.

## Exploration

- Mariner 10, launched by NASA, flew past Mercury three times in 1974 and 1975 and imaged about 45% of its surface.
- MESSENGER, launched by NASA in 2004, entered orbit around Mercury in March 2011, the first spacecraft to do so. It mapped the whole planet and detected the polar ice. The mission ended on 30 April 2015 when the spacecraft ran out of fuel and crashed onto the surface.
- BepiColombo, a joint mission of ESA and JAXA, was launched on 20 October 2018. It carries two orbiters, the Mercury Planetary Orbiter and the Mercury Magnetospheric Orbiter (Mio). After repeated flybys of Earth, Venus and Mercury, it is scheduled to enter orbit around Mercury in November 2026.

## Notable facts

- Mercury is smaller than Jupiter's moon Ganymede and Saturn's moon Titan, although it is much more massive than either.
- Because Mercury's day is longer than its year, the Sun can appear to stop and move backward in the Mercurian sky near the closest point of its orbit.
- Mercury is hard to see from Earth because it never strays far from the Sun in the sky. It is best seen shortly after sunset or before sunrise.
